Your brain under abuse

Chronic psychological abuse can produce real nervous-system changes similar to complex trauma: a hyper-reactive amygdala (hypervigilance), a stressed hippocampus (brain fog and fragmented memory), and a tired prefrontal cortex (harder decisions and emotional regulation).

That is why “just leave” or “just think clearly” can feel impossible. It is not weakness.

Hope: the brain can recover with safety, regulation, time, and trauma-informed support (EMDR, somatic work, trauma-focused therapy).

Stay sane while undecided

Gray Rock: short, dull, factual answers. No emotion, no extra information, no debate.

Yellow Rock: the same protection with polite, court-safe wording when children or logistics require contact.

These are temporary survival tools, not a life plan. Keep a secret support person, document facts on a safe device, protect sleep and tiny identity-rebuilding habits, and assume shared devices may be watched.

🔗

Trauma bonds

A trauma bond is attachment built by intermittent reinforcement — warmth then cruelty — plus a power imbalance. It lives in the nervous system, not just the mind.

Break it by naming it, interrupting contact, keeping a reality journal, grieving the fantasy, regulating first, and rebuilding with safe people. Returning more than once is common and not a personal failure.

🚪

Leave safely

Leaving can be the most dangerous time. Do not announce the plan. Work with 1800RESPECT or a local DV service on a personalised safety plan.

Quietly gather IDs, money, meds, keys, and a destination. Prefer leaving when they are not home. After leaving: block contact, document attempts, and ask about protection orders (ADVOs in NSW).

Expect hoovering

Hoovering is an attempt to pull you back after distance: sudden apologies, “I’ve changed”, crises, gifts, nostalgia, flying monkeys, or low-effort “hey” texts.

The strongest response is no response. Decide that before the first message arrives. Read your reality notes instead of answering.
